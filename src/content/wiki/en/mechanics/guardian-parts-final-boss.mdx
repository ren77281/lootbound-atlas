## How does the Guardian system work in Lootbound?

Lootbound sends the party through three biomes. At the end of each biome, the player chooses between two boss parts. Each selected part changes the appearance and power of the Guardian faced at the end of the expedition. After the third choice, the three decisions combine into the final boss.

This reverses the usual roguelite problem. You are not only building a party to answer a fixed boss; you are also deciding which boss properties that party will need to answer later. A part that looks harmless by itself can become the wrong choice when combined with the other two.

## How should you choose a Guardian part?

Choose the part your current damage engine is best equipped to handle, not the one that appears weakest in isolation. First identify whether the party wins by removing armor, bypassing armor, or scaling damage after armor is gone. Then compare the two offered parts against that plan.

| Current plan              | Part property that is easier to accept          | Part property to treat cautiously      | Why                                                                            |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Reliable Acid damage      | Additional armor                                | Additional health                      | Acid is described as reaching health directly instead of breaking armor first. |
| Slash and critical damage | Additional health after armor is solved         | Additional armor                       | Slash needs access to health, so more armor delays the build's main output.    |
| Crush into Slash          | A property the Crush package can remove         | A property not covered by the finisher | The build needs both the opener and health damage to remain functional.        |
| Mixed or unfinished build | The option that preserves the clearest fallback | Any part requiring a future item       | A hypothetical counter is not useful if the next biome never supplies it.      |

The Acid-versus-armor and Slash-versus-armor comparison is a tactical heuristic from launch coverage, not a complete part tier list. Exact Guardian values may change, and a party with weak Acid output can still struggle even if the damage type bypasses armor.

## What should you check at the first Guardian choice?

The first choice should establish a direction without locking the run to one missing reward. At this point two biomes remain, so there is time to strengthen a counter or pivot, but the party still needs to survive ordinary encounters.

Ask:

1. What damage type is already producing reliable results?
2. Which character carries that damage, and can its inventory still grow?
3. Does either part attack a weakness the party has already demonstrated?
4. If the preferred counter never drops, what is the fallback?

Do not select a part because a future legendary item would solve it. Select it because the current core can handle it or because the remaining route offers several realistic ways to close the gap.

## How should the second and third choices differ?

The second choice is where combinations become more important than individual parts. Re-evaluate the first selection together with the current offer and ask whether the two properties multiply the same weakness. A party that can manage one armor-focused part may still fail if the second choice adds another delay before health damage begins.

The third choice is a readiness check. There is no later biome in which to repair a speculative plan, so prefer the option the current inventories can answer immediately. Stop valuing theoretical ceiling and inspect the real party:

- Is the main damage engine complete and compact?
- Does each companion's inventory support its actual combat role?
- Is enough health preserved for the final encounter?
- Is enough mana available for abilities that the plan requires?
- Are consumables still on the player-controlled hero who can use them?

The [companion system guide](/mechanics/companion-system) explains why AI-controlled companions and their separate inventories must be prepared before combat rather than fixed through manual commands during a turn.

## How do resources affect Guardian preparation?

Armor replenishes after each fight in current public coverage, while health does not automatically return. Mana only partially regenerates between fights and recovers less on higher difficulties. That makes health and mana expedition resources rather than values to spend freely in every room.

Approaching the Guardian with the correct damage type but an empty resource pool is still a failed plan. Save ability-heavy sequences for fights that need them, use route recovery opportunities to protect health, and avoid taking unnecessary damage merely to test a new item before the final biome ends.

The [first-run guide](/guides/first-run-guide) covers route choices, campfires, blacksmith upgrades, and the D20 training-room risk that can affect final preparation.

## What are the most common Guardian planning mistakes?

### Choosing the least frightening-looking part

Appearance is not the decision rule. Translate the offered property into what your build must do during the final fight, then select the one your existing damage engine already counters.

### Treating each choice independently

Three individually manageable parts can combine into one bad matchup. Review all previous choices before confirming the next one.

### Believing the build is specialized when it is not

A few Acid or Slash bonuses do not prove the party has a functioning engine. Check which attacks actually produce the result and whether the relevant inventory survives a small rearrangement.

### Spending mana throughout the biome

Basic attacks do not consume mana, while abilities do. Using every ability in routine encounters can leave a sound Guardian plan without the resources needed to execute it.

### Forgetting to rebuild after the last reward

Adding one item changes the neighborhood around it. Re-check aura eligibility, awkward gaps, and every companion inventory before entering the final encounter.

## What is the final pre-fight checklist?

Use a short inspection rather than making one last speculative pivot:

1. Name the Guardian property that most threatens the party.
2. Identify the exact character and damage source that answers it.
3. Confirm that the answering items are active in the inventory, not held in reserve.
4. Move aura sources so their eligible beneficiaries actually touch them.
5. Remove filler that blocks a stronger compact layout.
6. Check health, mana, and usable consumables.
7. Enter with one coherent plan instead of several incomplete themes.

For the layout side of this inspection, use the [early equipment synergy guide](/items/early-equipment-synergies) and [inventory grid guide](/mechanics/inventory-grid-item-rotation).
