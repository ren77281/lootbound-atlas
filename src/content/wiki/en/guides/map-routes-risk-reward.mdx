## How should you choose a Lootbound route?

Pause at the branch and name the limiting factor: weak output, low durability, poor space efficiency, missing synergy, or excessive uncertainty. Prefer the room that addresses that constraint. A route that delays it needs enough upside or flexibility to justify the opportunity cost.

## How can you compare two branches?

Score each branch in three layers instead of looking only at its largest reward. The strongest route often has a useful floor and several follow-up options, even when another branch offers a more exciting ceiling.

| Layer | Question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate value | What important problem can this room fix now? |
| Option value | Does the result keep several future plans open? |
| Failure cost | Can the run continue if the outcome is poor? |

## When is extra risk justified?

Higher variance is rational when a safe route is unlikely to create enough power for the next major check, or when the current build has enough redundancy to survive a poor result. It is less attractive when one failure would dismantle a coherent setup.

Use the [dice mechanics guide](/mechanics/dice-roll-mechanics) to separate decision quality from the final roll.

## How do you build a useful route log?

Write down the alternatives and your reason before seeing the outcome. That preserves the information available at decision time and prevents a lucky result from turning a weak choice into a false rule.

1. Record the chosen branch and visible alternatives.
2. State why the room matched the current build.
3. Record the result without rewriting the original reasoning.
4. Separate a poor decision from a reasonable decision with a poor roll.
