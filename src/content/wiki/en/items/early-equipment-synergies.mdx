## How do you find an early equipment synergy?

Classify each piece by its job: engine, multiplier, defense, consistency, or flexible filler. A useful early pairing usually combines an engine with something that makes it trigger more often or operate more safely. Judge the pair by what it does now, not only by the build it might become later.

## What is the two-piece test?

The two-piece test checks whether an interaction is functional, flexible, and affordable before more rewards arrive. A pair that requires several missing pieces should remain a speculative direction with a reliable fallback.

- Can both pieces fit without consuming the entire pack?
- Is the interaction active immediately?
- Does the pair survive a rotation or small layout change?
- Can a third piece improve it without becoming mandatory?

Use the [aura and adjacency guide](/mechanics/item-aura-adjacency) when the pairing depends on exact placement.

## How do you compare synergy density?

Inventory space creates a hidden cost. Compare how much of the build's goal a cluster achieves per cell and how many awkward gaps it leaves. A smaller, flexible cluster may outperform a stronger-looking setup because it can accept better future rewards without a full rebuild.

## How does a promising run become evidence?

Capture the tooltips and full final layout, record the game version and date, and describe the activation order without guessing hidden rules. Repeat the key interaction before calling it consistent. This evidence can later support a specific build page without turning one unusual run into a universal recommendation.
